Focus & Interests

My main research area focuses on Analytic Number Theory. I am particularly interested in the asymptotic behavior of arithmetic functions and the use of complex analysis tools to solve counting problems. More specifically, my interests can be divided into four main areas:

Complex Analysis

Riemann Zeta Function ($\zeta(s)$), Transcendental Equations, and Holomorphic Dynamics.

Number Theory

Sieve methods, distribution of prime numbers, and additive problems.

Combinatorics

Enumerative, Analytic, and Symbolic Combinatorics. Connections with generating functions.

Probability

Applications of probability to Analytic Number Theory, Random Matrices, and their unreasonable effectiveness.

Questions I'm interested in

  • How can the Circle Method be generalized?
  • What is the intrinsic relationship between probability and number theory?
  • How is the Siewert-Burniston method applicable in different contexts?

Theses

B.Sc. Thesis: El Método del Círculo de Hardy–Littlewood

Facultad de Ciencias, UNAM (2025).
A historical study of the Hardy–Littlewood Circle Method, beginning with additive equations and the use of generating functions to count representations of an integer as a sum of elements from a prescribed set. The thesis traces the motivations behind the development of the Circle Method, its historical evolution, and its progression toward the modern formulation of the technique.

Master's Thesis: A Probabilistic Approach to the Bateman–Horn Conjecture

I am working on applying the ideas behind the Zeta distribution to the study of the Bateman–Horn conjecture, exploring how far this probabilistic approach can be developed and what results can be obtained from it.
