How can the analytic information in a Dirichlet series yield a formula for an arithmetic sum? The talk introduces the Riemann zeta function, Dirichlet series, and Perron’s formula. Contour shifts and the residue theorem provide a common approach to three examples.

The first two examples are the summatory functions of the Möbius and Liouville functions, associated with the Mertens and Pólya conjectures. Starting from their Dirichlet series, the talk explores how the zeros of the zeta function enter explicit formulas and the oscillations of these sums. It discusses their connection with the Riemann hypothesis and the disproof of both conjectures.

The third example is the Gauss circle problem, which counts lattice points inside a circle. Jacobi’s two-square theorem expresses the corresponding Dirichlet series in terms of the zeta function and the Dirichlet beta function. Their functional equations lead to a formula involving Bessel functions that describes the error term and its oscillations.
